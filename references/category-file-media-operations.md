# Category brief — File & Media Operations

Two slots, both cleared at **0/5 solved** — the best pair of results in any category. Both
needed multiple designs, and together they settle a question this category kept raising.

| | sub-category | final | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Media-1** | Batch rename & organization | 0/5 · 5 good-valid | **v7** — six versions each measured 2/2 solved before one held |
| **Media-2** | Archiving & compression | 0/5 · 5 good-valid | blocked 2/2 twice, then cleared once a constructive leg was added |

---

## The settled question: this category is not ceiling-limited

Media-1 hit **2/2 solved on six consecutive versions**. Media-2 was blocked 2/2 twice and was
filed at one point as *"too easy, not patchable"*. Both verdicts were wrong, and the corpus
carried the stale one for a while.

> **A 2/2 block here is a design problem, not a dead category.** Both slots ultimately reached
> 0/5 — the strongest possible gate result.

---

## What both slots needed, stated as one rule

**A constructive leg.** Media-2's own post-mortem is the clearest statement of it:

> A forced one-pass deterministic simulation cannot clear the gate, because **the agent's own
> replay is a perfect oracle for its own work** — correct transcription *is* the whole task.

Media-2's first design deliberately omitted a constructive leg (to stay clear of Media-1's
parking and eviction mechanics on similarity grounds) and **that omission is exactly what
failed.** Adding one cleared it.

Media-1's kills, when they finally came, were from **chained eviction** — a construction agents
never discovered — not from its layout beliefs.

The general form (§3.1): every task that reached 0/5 has one of three things — a narrowing
belief search cannot cross, **a construction agents never find**, or extreme feasible density.
If your design has none of the three, that is structural, not tunable.

---

## The law Media-1 contributed: legality, not naming

Six versions died because a wrong belief only ever **misnamed** the artifact. Version 7 changed
one thing: a wrong belief made a plan **ILLEGAL**.

> **Difficulty must live in what a wrong belief makes impossible — never in what it mislabels.**

Concretely, that means an operation ORDER that becomes impossible, or a stated scarce resource
sized to the correct schedule's high-water mark. File and media work is magnetically attracted
to the naming shape — "classify these assets", "name the output correctly", "parse this
container format" — and all of it is dead.

---

## Similarity between the two slots

Media-2 avoided Media-1's parking and eviction mechanics deliberately, which is correct
practice — and then discovered that the mechanic it avoided *was the source of the difficulty*.
The resolution was not to reuse it but to build a **different** construction.

> When similarity forces you off a mechanic, replace it with another **construction** — not with
> a checked property.

---

## Pre-build checklist

1. **Is there a construction the agent must find**, or only a transcription to perform?
2. **Can the agent's own replay verify its own work?** Then you have a perfect self-oracle and
   the task is transcription.
3. **Does a wrong belief make something ILLEGAL, or only misnamed?**
4. **Does your design have one of the three 0/5 ingredients** — an uncrossable narrowing belief,
   an undiscovered construction, or extreme feasible density?
5. **Are you avoiding a mechanic on similarity grounds?** Check whether that mechanic *was* the
   difficulty; if so, find a different construction rather than a checked property.
6. **Is the scarce resource sized to the correct schedule's high-water mark?**
7. **Is a fatal move unrecoverable and not locally visible?**
8. **Exact integers in the graded path.**
9. **Blind public sample** — passes for wrong-belief archetypes.
10. **If you are at 2/2 twice, redesign the leg — do not abandon the category.**

---

## Honest limits

Two slots, but **thirteen versions between them** — this category has the worst
versions-per-clear ratio in the corpus. Both cleared at 0/5 in the end, so the ceiling is real
headroom; the cost is in finding the construction.

Eight sub-categories are untouched: file search and filtering, file format conversion, text
editing and manipulation, file permissions and metadata, recovery and repair, video processing,
audio and music processing, and image and design processing. **Recovery and repair** is the
most promising of those on this category's own evidence — reconstruction is inherently
constructive.
