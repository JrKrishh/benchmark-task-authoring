# Category brief — Data Querying & Databases

One slot, cleared on its first attempt. Short brief, honestly — but it carries one law worth
more than its size.

| | sub-category | final |
|---|---|---|
| **DataQuery-1** | SQL querying | 1/5 · 4 good-valid · avg 0.200 |

---

## The law this slot contributed: grain is inert under summation

> **A belief that only changes the GRAIN of an aggregate can never change a folded total,
> because summation is associative.**

A finer intermediate rolls up to exactly the same number as a coarser one. The belief moves the
code path and the artifact's *label* while every graded figure stays correct.

Measured, with three archetypes run as full planners over 480 reports:

| archetype | reports where it bit | **wrong number** | crash | *identical number* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| one-pass instead of fixpoint | 142 | **0** | 0 | 142 |
| route-blind absorption | 161 | **0** | 84 | 77 |
| companion without its grain requirement | 457 | **0** | 0 | 457 |

**Zero wrong numbers across 760 bites.** The slot's entire original core was *"what resolution
must this intermediate be materialized at?"* — and that question is invisible to a sum.

This is the inert-belief law (§5.4) in its sharpest form: **an archetype can be
symmetry-equivalent to the truth and kill 0/12.** Before building on any belief, ask whether the
grading operation is associative or commutative over the thing the belief changes. If it is,
the belief is inert no matter how wrong it looks.

What cleared it instead was making the fixed point converge to the **wrong end** — all three
analytical failures were the same line: resolved forms initialised to the *finest* grain instead
of the coarsest, halting the iteration at a wrong self-consistent fixed point. The public queue
never exercised the feedback scenario, so public tests passed for every failing trial while all
12 sealed tests failed.

---

## The second finding: write archetypes as an agent would write them

The archetypes that mattered were **full planners**, not patched predicates — the same discipline
the archetype-faithfulness law states (§5.4 and the games brief). A belief has to be threaded
through every site that reads it, or one correct site quietly restores the right answer.

---

## Category hazards

**"Write the correct query" is transcription.** SQL is the most documented query language there
is; a task whose core is producing the right SELECT is a naming task, not a construction.

**Query-plan and optimisation framing hits the dead margin law** (§3.1 #4).

**Anything expressible as rules-applied-to-rows factorises** (§5.2) — and this category hands
you rows.

**Watch the engine.** If your design leans on a real database, name it and check it against the
approved base-image list before proposing. If a real engine can answer the question, the agent
will install one.

---

## What worked, as a shape

A **fixed-point over interdependent properties** — repeatedly applying promotion, absorption,
resolution and lift as one coordinated round, each round feeding the next — with grouping and
materialization downstream of the converged identity. That is computed object identity (§5.2)
in database clothing, and it is the shape to reach for here.

---

## Pre-build checklist

1. **Is the grading operation associative or commutative** over what your belief changes? Then
   the belief is inert.
2. **Is the core "write the correct query"?** That is transcription.
3. **Are you grading a query-plan margin?** Dead.
4. **Do your rules apply per row?** Then they factorise.
5. **Does a real engine answer the question?** The agent will install it.
6. **Are your archetypes full planners**, threaded through every site?
7. **Does the public queue exercise the feedback scenario?** It should NOT — that blindness is
   what made this slot's kills land.
8. **Does the fixed point have a wrong end to converge to?**
9. **Exact integers in the graded path.**
10. **`n_items × per_item_cap < verifier timeout_sec`.**

---

## Honest limits

**n = 1.** Everything above except the grain law is a single observation. The grain law itself
is well-measured (760 bites, zero wrong numbers) and generalises beyond this category.

Five sub-categories are untouched: analytical queries, query optimization, database
administration, NoSQL and document stores, and graph/semantic queries. **Query optimization
carries the margin hazard by name.** Graph and semantic queries are the most promising
unexplored option — graph reachability and closure have constructive shapes that do not reduce
to a sum.
