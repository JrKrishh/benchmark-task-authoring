# Category brief — Hardware, Embedded & Low-Level Systems

Three slots, **all three cleared**, but this is the category with the most designs killed on the
way — and the one where the *environment* constrains you hardest.

| | sub-category | final | what it taught |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LowLevel-1** | GPU kernels & accelerators | 1/5 | 16/16 checks; the wall-clock-under-contention law |
| **LowLevel-2** | GPU kernels & accelerators (2nd) | 1/5 · 4 good-valid | the checked→constructed **positive control** |
| **LowLevel-3** | Low-level runtime & performance | 2/5 · 3 good-valid · avg 0.400 | six designs; two of the manual's core laws came from killing them |

---

## The category's four standing constraints

These are not design preferences; they are properties of the domain that hold for every slot.

**Everything is NAMED.** Cache lines, TLBs, warps, wavefronts, page tables, store buffers,
memory fences — every mechanism has a canonical name and a textbook description the model
holds. **Lexical or reading difficulty is dead here** (§3.1 #5, and the reading-vs-implementation
A/B). If your crux can be looked up, it is knowledge.

**Optimization margin is dead.** Do not build "get within X% of optimal" (§3.1 #4).

**No floats in the graded path.** Rejection risk, and slack an agent can be wrong inside.

**There is no real accelerator.** The machine must be *modelled* in the spec, which has a
mechanical consequence: `instruction.md` is capped at **1,500 tokens**, so a bespoke ISA cannot
fit inline. Ship the model as a referenced file (`task/environment/data/SPEC.md`).

> ⚠️ **And the referenced spec must not become an oracle.** Measured elsewhere: a worked example
> let a winning agent converge by *diffing intermediates* rather than deriving. Ship the public
> instance with **no derivation and no expected answer**, and assert that in a spec check.

## The positive control this category owns

LowLevel-2 is the cleanest evidence anywhere that **checked predicates cannot carry difficulty**
(§5.1) — because it is a controlled experiment rather than two unrelated tasks.

| | design | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| #1 | core was *"is this value uniform on the active set"* — a **checked** predicate | killed pre-build: undecidable over all inputs, or trivial by simulation over a shipped bundle, or transcription by a stated calculus |
| #2 | same author, same slot, same sub-category, **same week** — only checked→constructed changed: the agent must construct a canonical layout through a 3-rule cascade, then a legal relocation | **pass@5 1/5 · 4 valid fails · `approach_validity` 5/5** |

One variable, opposite results. Note also why the escape hatch was blocked: SAT/BDD solvers
would have made the analysis trivial, but `allow_internet = true` is mandated so the agent
simply installs one — **you cannot design around solver availability in this category.**

## Two laws came out of LowLevel-3's six dead designs

**Product-vs-state-graph (§3.1 #3).** Designs #1 and #2 were both products of per-item ranges —
assign items positions under a cumulative resource. Both were killed on paper the same day:
one-line policies won 85–95% of instances. The tell that replaced them: **what does a move
destroy?** Difficulty needs a state graph reached by *destructive* moves, where the resource is
consumed and regenerated by the moves themselves.

**Belief-rate (§5.5), and it is the more uncomfortable one.** LowLevel-3 shipped a fully green
local pipeline — pooled kill-given-belief **≥ 98.46%** at n ≥ 235, variants 26/26, rubric 31/31
first roll — and went **pass@5 5/5 SOLVED, avg 1.000.**

Measured belief rate across 17 live trials: 15 solves, 1 kill, 1 crash. Only 2 of 7 trials
engaged the wrong walk at all; one shipped it; one self-identified and fixed it at step 30.

> **A single stated rule, however misreadable in principle, is implemented correctly by careful
> strong agents about 85% of the time.** The battery measures kill-GIVEN-belief; the gate
> measures belief RATE; they are unrelated. **Depth of interacting cases — not strength of a
> single wrong prior — is what converts stated rules into failures.**

What finally cleared LowLevel-3 was **re-selecting instances of an already-built design against
the archetype that had solved it** — not a new core. When your design survives the kill-list but
agents still solve it, the instances are the repair (§6.4).

**A third finding worth keeping:** the kill that lands is often not the crux you designed.
LowLevel-3's one real kill came from a rule never designed as a crux and never batteried, while
its *designed* crux was engaged and solved by every agent — *"the instruction states the walk so
explicitly that re-reading repairs it."* **When a real slip shows up in a probe, promote it into
a designed, mirrored, seeded family instead of leaving it to luck.**

## Similarity inside this category

LowLevel-1 and LowLevel-2 share a sub-category, so differentiation was binding. What was
required, and what passed: differ in **counted quantity, emitted artifact, semantic core and
coupling mechanism**. LowLevel-1 partitioned an op list into passes with coupling =
*simultaneity*; LowLevel-2 could not ship another partition-and-count and moved to construction
plus relocation.

The wall-clock law also lives here (LowLevel-1): **bar the naive-search archetype in wall clock
at the disclosed cap, measured un-contended.** Contention on your own machine makes a
naive solver look slower than it is.

## Pre-build checklist

1. **Can your crux be looked up?** In this domain almost everything can. If yes, it is knowledge.
2. **Is the core a checked predicate** (uniformity, aliasing, escape, "does a legal X exist")?
   Force a construction instead — see the positive control above.
3. **What does a move destroy?** If nothing, you have a product of ranges and a solver eats it.
4. **Are you grading a margin?** Dead here.
5. **Any floats in the graded path?**
6. **Does the machine model fit outside `instruction.md`'s 1,500 tokens**, as a referenced spec
   with no worked derivation and no expected answer?
7. **Is your difficulty one stated rule, or many interacting cases?** One rule gets implemented
   correctly ~85% of the time.
8. **Have you measured belief RATE, not just kill-given-belief?** Probe before pushing.
9. **Is the naive-search family barred in wall clock at the disclosed cap, un-contended?**
10. **Sharing a sub-category with an earlier slot?** Differ in all four axes, not just the story.

---

## Honest limits

Three slots, three clears — but LowLevel-3 alone burned six designs and one full 5/5-solved
gate measurement to get there, and two of its designs were killed on paper *after* being drafted.
This is the most expensive category in the corpus per cleared slot.

Two of three slots are GPU kernels; the record for the other four sub-categories is one slot
(low-level runtime) or none at all — embedded and firmware, RTL and digital design, DSP and
signal hardware, and CAD and mechanical workflows are entirely unmeasured. Those last two in
particular may not share this category's "everything is named" property.
